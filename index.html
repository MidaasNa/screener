The 10% Momentum + Pullback system

The goal is not “10% every week.” The goal is to only take a trade when trend, momentum, volume, structure and sector all line up and the reward is at least twice the risk. When nothing qualifies, the correct output is “no trade.”

Hard gate: any setup with reward-to-risk below 2:1 is rejected regardless of score.

Entry, stop, target

  • Entry — the breakout level, ideally bought on the retest rather than chased on the breakout candle.
  • Stop — just under the support / consolidation low. If no support is given, the tool uses a 3% stop for Daily and 5% for Weekly timeframe.
  • Targets — scale out: ~25% at +5%, ~25–30% at +7.5%, the rest near +10%, then trail any remainder only if momentum is exceptional.

Position sizing

Risk a fixed 1% of capital per trade. Shares = (capital × 1%) ÷ (entry − stop), capped so you never deploy more than your capital. One bad trade should cost 1%, not 10%.

The break-even exit — why you keep leaving 10-baggers on the table

Holding LAURUSLABS through −50%, then selling the moment it got back to ~700, then watching it go to 1900 — that is the disposition effect, the most studied and most expensive mistake in retail trading: you hold losers hoping to get back to even, and you sell winners the instant the pain of the earlier loss is gone. The relief of "I didn't lose money" overwhelms the fact that you're sitting in a stock that is still trending up.

Three things went wrong, and each has a fix:

  • No stop at entry → you rode −50%. Fix: the stop is set the moment you buy (1% of capital), and it is never widened. If it hits, you're out, and you can always re-buy later on a clean setup with no baggage.
  • The exit was a feeling, not a rule → "finally green, get out." Fix: your exit is a price (a trailing stop that follows the structure up) or a signal (a close below the 50-EMA on heavy volume). Both are decided in advance. Your P&L relative to your cost is not an input.
  • You anchored to your buy price → 700 felt like "the top" because it was your cost. Fix: 700 was actually a breakout from a base — a place to add, not sell. The stock doesn't know or care what you paid.

The tool now has a "Hold vs Exit" panel (Analyzer, below the chart). For any stock you hold it shows: is the trend intact, where the trailing stop should be right now (from price structure, not your entry), a measured-move and trend-extension target, and — from that stock's own 2-year history — the probability it reaches +10% / +20% / +30% / +50% from a spot like this, plus the odds of an 8% shakeout first. When the trend is intact the answer is HOLD and trail; the panel makes that concrete so the decision stops being emotional. Enter your average buy price if you want (it's shown for context and then explicitly ignored).

The uncomfortable truth: with the Stage-2 continuation pattern, your winners are supposed to be up 30–100%+ while your losers are −5%. If you cut winners at +2–5%, you have personally inverted the only edge the setup has. One 10-bagger held properly pays for a year of small stops.

Stage-2 continuation — catching them before the big move

The most repeatable big winners (LAURUSLABS, TITAN, and the like) share one shape: a stock already in a Stage-2 advance that pauses in a tight, shallow consolidation riding a rising moving average, then resumes. The pause is where you get in — not the vertical part.

What every one of them has in common:

  • Already trending — price above a rising 50- and 200-EMA, within ~15% of the 52-week high, well off the 52-week low. The tool scores this 0–7 (the Stage-2 number); 5+ qualifies.
  • Leadership — the stock is outperforming the pack. RS rank 80+ is the single strongest "something is going on here" signal. Relative strength shows up on the chart before the news does.
  • Tight, shallow bases — each pause is ≤10–15% deep and a few weeks long, with volume drying up inside it.
  • The base sits on a rising EMA — the 20 or 50 catches up to price during the pause, then acts as the launchpad.
  • Higher lows / stacking — each base forms above the last one (base-on-base). Volatility contracts base to base (the ⋀ mark).
  • Volume expands on the break — ≥1.5× average as it clears the base.

What actually made them move was a catalyst — an earnings beat, guidance upgrade, a sector re-rating (Laurus = pharma CDMO cycle, Titan = jewellery/consumption), sometimes index inclusion or a large fund building a position. You usually can't know the catalyst in advance. But the accumulation footprint it leaves — rising RS, tight bases on the MA, volume signature — is screenable, and that's what this does.

Is it "the most profitable"? It's the highest-expectancy swing pattern in the literature (Minervini's SEPA, O'Neil's CAN SLIM, Weinstein's Stage Analysis, Darvas boxes all describe it) — in a trending market. It is not a high win-rate setup; the edge is that winners run 20–50%+ while you cut base failures at −3 to −5%. Your personal results depend on entering only A-grade bases, cutting fast, and standing aside in corrections.

The trigger — a reversal candle at the moving average

The pause resolves off a specific bar. On the pullback setups the resumption very often begins with a hammer, bullish pin bar, inverted hammer or bullish engulfing candle whose low tags the rising 20- or 50-EMA and closes back above it. That candle is the "buyers just took the MA back" signal — it's the entry, with the stop going just under that candle's low.

The tool now detects this. When a trigger candle has printed at the rising MA in the last few bars, the Setup cell shows it (e.g. pullback→MA · 🔨 hammer today · 58%). The 58% is a genuine cross-check: how often, over this stock's own last 2 years, a trigger candle at a rising MA in a Stage-2 trend was followed by price tagging +6% before −3%. Use the "Triggered" filter to see only names where the candle has just fired — those are the "buy tomorrow" candidates. On the chart, the trigger bar is marked with a small ▲.

Your instinct is right that the move usually starts on one of these candles — but the candle without the context is a coin-flip. It only carries an edge when it prints at a rising MA, in a Stage-2 leader (RS 80+), inside a tight base. That's why it's a confirmation of the setup, not a setup on its own.

Is he really getting 20–100% in days/weeks, repeatedly? Some of it is real and some of it is how it's being counted:

  • The pattern genuinely produces violent moves — a low-float small-cap at a new high with a supply vacuum above it and a fresh volume thrust can add 30–60% in a few weeks. That's not a trick; it's what thin floats do when demand shows up and there are no trapped sellers overhead.
  • Selection / survivorship bias — you see the ones that worked. A screen like his throws off many candidates; the losers get stopped out at −1 to −5% and are never posted. The winners are screenshotted. The honest scorecard is "many small losses, occasional very large winner" — the average trade is positive only if you actually cut the losers at 1% and actually hold the winners.
  • Bull-market regime — this style only works when the broader market is trending. In a correction, ATH breakouts fail in bulk. The posts cluster in strong markets.
  • Low-float = two-edged — the same thinness that makes them fly makes them gap down just as hard, and makes them targets for pump groups. Slippage on entry and exit is real. Position size (his capital ÷ 10) is doing a lot of the risk control.
  • Compounding math — "12 trades at +9% each turns 20L into 56L" is arithmetically true and psychologically misleading; it assumes no losing trades between the winners, which never happens.

What to actually do with it here: Scan (with Fetch latest quarterly results on), then look for Setup = HDF or the ATH badge with RS ≥ 80, Stage-2 ≥ 5, a Results badge of ▲ or ★, and a fresh trigger. Open the chart, confirm the thrust → shallow correction → tiny-candle base shape by eye, check the Fib retracement is shallow or in the golden zone. The tool now grades the last reported quarter for you — you still verify free float and that the result is clean (not one-off / other-income) on screener.in. Enter on a buy-stop above the prior day's high, stop 1% below, and let the Hold vs Exit panel — not your P&L — tell you when to leave.

The extra columns

  • Base — the consolidation the stock is breaking out of, shown as width % × bars. The scanner walks back from a few bars ago and measures how tight and how long the sideways range was, then checks where price sits relative to the top of it:
    • VCP — a very tight base (≤7.5% wide, ≥10 bars) breaking out on ≥1.5× volume. The highest-quality pattern (Volatility Contraction). Scores the full 20 on Structure.
    • tight break / base break — a tight base (≤12%) breaking out, with less volume confirmation.
    • coiling — price is still inside a tight base, near the top. Not a trade yet — set an alert above the base high.
    • wide break — broke out but the base was loose (>12%); lower conviction.
    • past base +x% — already extended above the base; you missed the entry, wait for a pullback.
    Use the “Base breakouts only” filter to see just the names clearing a base right now.
  • Prob* — estimated chance of tagging the first target (+5%) before the stop. For a scanned stock it's a historical follow-through rate: how often, over the last 2 years, this stock reached +5% before −stop% within the holding window when it was in a similar state (price above rising EMAs, RSI 50–74). A trailing e means there wasn't enough history and it fell back to a score-based estimate. It is a base rate, not a forecast.
  • Support / Resist — the nearest swing pivot below and above price (fractal highs/lows plus the rising 20-EMA). “sky” = price is at/near its highs with no overhead pivot.
  • Expectancy (Analyzer) — P(+10%) × R:R − (1 − P(+10%)), in units of R (your 1% risk). Positive = the setup pays over many repetitions; it says nothing about the next single trade.
  • Results — the latest reported quarter's year-on-year growth. The scan pulls ~5 quarters of revenue / net income / diluted EPS from Yahoo's fundamentals feed (through the same proxy) and matches the current quarter to the one ~a year earlier (Yahoo occasionally skips a quarter, so it date-matches rather than counting back 4). The badge grades it — ★ strong (profit +25%+ YoY and sales growing), ▲ growing, · flat, ▼ shrinking, – no data — and the number shown is profit/EPS YoY; hover for the sales figure. A ▼ blocks the A+ grade.
    • Timing. Yahoo only gives the quarter-end date, not the announcement date — so the tool estimates: NSE companies must file within ~45 days of quarter-end (SEBI LODR), and it adds ~40 days. The cell shows roughly how long ago that was (· 3w), and the Analyzer names the estimated report month. The exact date is on screener.in / your broker — this is a guide, not a calendar.
    • Next result due. When the estimate puts the next quarterly result within ~3 weeks, the cell shows ⚠ result ~Nd and the summary bar shows N result due. A swing entry then holds through a binary event — the playbook says wait until after, or size down hard.
    • Market expectation (shown on the ⚠ hover and in the Analyzer). With no analyst-estimate feed available for NSE, the tool infers the lean from two things it can see: the earnings trajectory — is YoY profit growth accelerating or fading quarter to quarter, and what is trailing-12-month profit doing — and how the stock is trading into the print — a Stage-2 leader at new highs with RS 80+ is the market positioning for a beat; a name breaking down with weak RS is bracing for a miss. Output: positive / leaning positive / neutral / leaning negative / negative. It is a read on positioning, not a prediction of the number.

Option chain

The open-interest picture does move stocks — but as a range and a sentiment tool, not a direction predictor. Two honest limits up front:

  • Data is manual. NSE serves the option chain only to browsers, not to servers — the Cloudflare Worker gets an empty response every time. So the Analyzer has a small Option chain panel (F&O stocks only) where you type ~6 numbers from your broker / Sensibull / Opstra: days to expiry, ATM implied volatility, max pain, the highest-Call-OI strike, the highest-Put-OI strike, and PCR. Takes about 20 seconds and they're saved per-stock in your browser.
  • Nothing predicts the next price. What the chain does give you: the expected-move range the options market is pricing for this expiry (spot × IV × √(days/365) — the same ±1σ band your broker shows), the OI walls (heaviest call strike ≈ resistance, heaviest put strike ≈ support), the max-pain magnet (only meaningful in expiry week), and a PCR sentiment read.

The panel then does the one genuinely useful thing: it checks whether the option structure confirms or fights the chart setup.

  • Is the +10% target realistic for the expiry? It shows how many σ of expected move the target is. Inside 1σ = a realistic reach in the window; beyond ~1.6σ = the options market is pricing that as unlikely before expiry, so the move needs more time than you're giving it.
  • Is there an OI wall in the way? If the heaviest call strike sits between your entry and your target, that's where writers defend hardest — price usually stalls there first. It's flagged, and drawn on the chart (red dashed = call wall, green dashed = put wall, grey = max pain, shaded band = expected move).
  • Which way is sentiment leaning? PCR > 1.2 with put-writing at a strike below = a floor being built (mild bullish); PCR < 0.8 with a fat call wall just overhead = capped (mild bearish). Extremes flip contrarian.

It outputs an options-implied view — bullish / leaning bullish / neutral / leaning bearish / bearish, with the ±1σ range — and one line: option chain backs the setup / is neutral / is a headwind. Important caveat the panel repeats: OI walls hold best in quiet markets and routinely break on exactly the momentum breakouts this tool screens for. Read "headwind" as "this needs conviction volume to work", not "don't take the trade". Use it to size and to pick a realistic target, not as a signal.

Getting the data in

Auto-screen (downloaded file only): set your universe and leading sectors, then “Scan now”. It pulls ~2 years of daily candles per stock (from Yahoo Finance, via a CORS proxy) and computes EMA 20/50/200, RSI(14), MACD, volume ratio and the breakout / support levels locally, then scores the whole list. Results cache for the day.

You need a CORS proxy. A browser can't call Yahoo directly (same-origin policy), and the free public proxies are almost all dead. The fix is to run your own — a Cloudflare Worker, 5 minutes, free forever, no keys:

  1. Sign up at cloudflare.com (free).
  2. Workers & Pages → Create → Worker → name it (e.g. ledger-proxy) → Deploy.
  3. Edit code, replace everything with: 
    export default {
  async fetch(req) {
    const u = new URL(req.url).searchParams.get("url");
    if (!u) return new Response("no url", { status: 400 });
    const r = await fetch(u, { headers: { "User-Agent": "Mozilla/5.0" } });
    const b = await r.arrayBuffer();
    return new Response(b, { status: r.status, headers: {
      "content-type": r.headers.get("content-type") || "application/json",
      "access-control-allow-origin": "*" } });
  }
};
  4. Deploy. Copy the URL it gives you (like https://ledger-proxy.you.workers.dev).
  5. Paste https://ledger-proxy.you.workers.dev/?url={url} into the Worker / proxy URL field on the Screener tab. (Keep the literal {url} — the tool swaps it per request.) It's saved in your browser.
  6. Test it works: open <your-url>/?url=https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/RELIANCE.NS in a browser tab — you should see JSON.

Cloudflare's free tier is 100,000 requests/day — you will never hit it. A Nifty-200 scan takes ~30 seconds for prices, ~60 with Fetch latest quarterly results on (it makes a second request per stock to Yahoo's fundamentals-timeseries feed — the pass-through Worker above handles it with no changes). If the results feed ever stops responding the scan gives up on it after a dozen misses and finishes on prices alone.

Or paste your own data from your broker terminal, TradingView, screener.in or a Chartink scan into the “Raw data” box.

The auto-screener's breakout / support / resistance / MACD / higher-high-low reads are rule-based approximations from the candle series — always eyeball the chart before acting.

Daily workflow

  • Pre-open — set the market regime from Nifty + sector action + overnight news.
  • First hour — do not chase opening spikes.
  • Mid-session — run the Screener; look for breakout + volume + retest.
  • Last hour — confirm the move is holding before entering.
  • After close — build tomorrow’s watchlist. Cap it at 1–2 A-grade trades a day.
